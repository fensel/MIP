What?

The vision of Minimal Interoperability Profiles (MIPs) is to make semantic interoperability practical, scalable, and accessible. MIPs define the smallest ontology-based semantic model required to enable meaningful data exchange and integration across domains. Rather than aiming to comprehensively model an entire domain, a MIP provides a lightweight starting point (10–15 classes and properties connecting at least 2 domains) that captures the concepts, relationships, and metadata needed for the most common interoperability scenarios.

As an example, an illustration of the encoded MIP example – for food and health domains – is presented in the Appendix, where the 1st image illustrates its possible ontology schema. Whenever possible, MIPs reuse established domain ontologies and Semantic Web standards (RDF/S, OWL, etc.), while allowing new ontology items to be developed where they are missing. Data and metadata that conform to a MIP can be represented as interoperable knowledge graphs, providing a machine-understandable semantic layer for FAIR and AI-ready data.

Why?

Many of today's scientific and societal challenges require combining data across disciplines that have evolved independently using different terminologies, data models, and standards. At WUR, this need spans food and health, agriculture, biodiversity, environmental monitoring, climate adaptation, water management, and the circular bioeconomy, where answering complex research questions increasingly depends on integrating diverse datasets.

Existing ontologies and interoperability standards provide rich semantic foundations but are often too extensive or complex for rapid adoption. MIPs lower this barrier by offering a common semantic entry point that enables communities to begin sharing interoperable data quickly, while allowing more detailed semantic models to be incorporated incrementally as requirements evolve.

How?

Our research develops the methodologies, ontology design patterns, engineering tools, and governance approaches needed to design, validate, implement, and maintain MIPs and MIP-compliant knowledge graphs. The approach combines ontology engineering, Linked Data, knowledge graph technologies, ontology alignment, SHACL-based validation, and AI-assisted semantic engineering to identify the minimum shared semantics needed for interoperability.

The resulting MIPs act as semantic application profiles that address the most common data exchange scenarios, while remaining compatible with existing ontologies and extensible towards richer domain models. This makes them particularly suitable for interdisciplinary research, federated data infrastructures, and data spaces, where organizations need practical interoperability without first harmonizing every aspect of their information models.

For whom and by whom?

This research is intended for domain scientists, knowledge engineers, data stewards, research infrastructures, public authorities, and industry partners seeking an accessible path to interoperable, reusable, and AI-ready data. At WUR, it contributes to strengthening interdisciplinary research by providing a common semantic foundation across research domains and supporting FAIR-by-design data infrastructures and data spaces such as CEADS.

The long-term ambition is to establish MIPs as a reusable semantic engineering methodology that enables research communities to rapidly bootstrap semantic interoperability, accelerate the creation of interoperable knowledge graphs, and provide a practical foundation for trustworthy AI and data-driven scientific discovery.

MIPs are built to help communities share data meaningfully across domains without needing to harmonize every underlying model from day one.
